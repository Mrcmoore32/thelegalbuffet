Copy, fill in, send

Fill-in-the-blank letter templates

Five plain-language letters you can copy, fill in, and send yourself. No account, no form, nothing collected — the letters live on this page for you to copy or print.

How to use any letter on this page: copy it into an email or a document, replace every [bracketed blank] with your own details, keep a copy for yourself, and send it in a way you can prove — certified mail with return receipt, or email where you keep the sent copy. A letter does not replace legal advice: if the other side threatens court, talks about a lawsuit, or the money is large, talk to a lawyer or your state’s free legal help first (see your state page).

Watch out for deadlines. Some rules in these letters differ by state — security-deposit return deadlines are the big one. Where a rule varies, the letter tells you to fill it in from your state page instead of printing one national number. Never copy another state’s deadline into your letter.

1. Debt-validation letter

Use it when: a debt collector contacts you about a debt you do not recognize, or you want proof before you pay anything. Send it soon after the collector first contacts you: under federal debt-collection law, you have 30 days from that first contact to ask for validation in writing. This letter does not erase a debt you actually owe — it makes the collector prove the debt is yours and the amount is right.

[Your full name]
[Your mailing address]
[Date]

[Collector name]
[Collector mailing address]

Re: Account number [account number, if shown on their letter] — request for validation

Dear [Collector name]:

I am writing in response to your letter dated [date of their letter] about a debt you say I owe. I dispute this debt and I am asking you to validate it.

Please send me written proof that includes: the name and address of the original creditor; the amount you claim I owe and how that amount was calculated; a copy of any agreement or judgment you say supports this debt; and proof that you are licensed to collect this debt in my state, if my state requires it.

Until you provide this validation, please stop all collection activity on this account, including phone calls. Please contact me only in writing at the address above.

Sincerely,
[Your full name]

2. Security-deposit demand

Use it when: you moved out and the landlord kept your deposit, or returned only part of it without a clear itemized list. Fill in your state’s deadline from your state page’s Tenant rights section — deposit-return deadlines are set by each state and are not the same everywhere, so do not copy another state’s number. Send it after the deadline in your state has passed, or as soon as you get an itemized list you disagree with.

[Your full name]
[Your forwarding mailing address]
[Date]

[Landlord or property manager name]
[Landlord mailing address]

Re: Security deposit for [rental address, apartment number] — demand for return

Dear [Landlord name]:

I moved out of [rental address] on [move-out date]. I paid a security deposit of $[amount]. Under [your state]’s law, the deposit was due back by [deadline under your state’s law — see your state page’s Tenant rights section], with an itemized list of any deductions.

As of today, I have [received nothing / received only $___ without a full itemized list / received an itemized list I disagree with for these reasons: ___]. I am asking you to return $[amount you are claiming] within [number] days of this letter.

If I do not receive the deposit or a full written explanation, I will pursue the remedies available to me, which may include filing in small claims court. Please send payment and any itemized list to the address above.

Sincerely,
[Your full name]
[Phone number, optional] [Email address, optional]

3. Landlord repair request

Use it when: something in your rental needs fixing — no heat, a leak, broken appliances, unsafe conditions — and telling the landlord in person or by phone has not worked. Put it in writing so there is a dated record. What counts as a repair the landlord must make, and whether you may withhold rent or pay for a repair yourself and deduct it, varies by state — check your state page’s Tenant rights section before you mention either option, and do not stop paying rent unless a lawyer or a tenant hotline in your state tells you it is allowed.

[Your full name]
[Your rental address, apartment number]
[Date]

[Landlord or property manager name]
[Landlord mailing address]

Re: Repair needed at [rental address, apartment number]

Dear [Landlord name]:

I am writing to report a problem that needs repair: [describe the problem clearly — what is broken, where it is, and when it started].

This problem affects [health / safety / use of the home — say how, for example: no hot water since (date), or a leak that is damaging the floor]. I first told you about it on [date] by [phone / text / in person], and it has not been fixed.

Please arrange the repair by [date you are asking for — pick a reasonable date, sooner for urgent problems like no heat or water]. Please let me know in writing when the repair will happen and how workers will get in. I am keeping a copy of this letter and photos of the problem.

Thank you,
[Your full name]
[Phone number] [Email address]

4. Wage-claim letter

Use it when: an employer owes you pay — unpaid hours, a missing final paycheck, unpaid overtime, or wages promised and never paid. Send it to the employer first, in writing, before or alongside a complaint to your state’s labor agency (find that agency on your state page’s Unpaid wages section). Wage-claim deadlines and procedures vary by state, so file promptly and do not wait for the employer to get around to it.

[Your full name]
[Your mailing address]
[Date]

[Employer name]
[Employer mailing address]

Re: Unpaid wages — [your job title, work location]

Dear [Employer name]:

I am writing about wages I am owed and have not been paid. I worked for you from [start date] to [end date, or “to the present”] as a [job title] at [work location].

The unpaid wages are: [list each item — for example: 12 hours worked March 3–7, 2026 at $___ per hour = $___; final paycheck for the pay period ending (date) = $___]. The total amount owed is $[total]. I have attached [pay stubs / time records / schedule / written pay agreement, if you have them].

Please pay the full amount of $[total] within [number] days of this letter. If I do not receive payment, I will file a wage claim with [your state’s labor agency — see your state page’s Unpaid wages section] and pursue any other remedies available to me.

Sincerely,
[Your full name]
[Phone number] [Email address]

5. Insurance-claim dispute letter

Use it when: your insurer denied your claim, paid less than the damage or loss, or is stalling. Your policy and the insurer’s letters set your appeal deadlines — fill those in from those documents, not from this template, and send this before that deadline passes. Attach your evidence (photos, estimates, receipts) and keep copies of everything you send. Your state page’s Disaster & insurance recovery section has your state’s insurance department if the insurer will not budge.

[Your full name]
[Your mailing address]
[Date]

[Insurance company name]
[Claims department mailing address]

Re: Claim number [claim number] — Policy number [policy number] — request for review

Dear Claims Department:

I am writing to dispute your decision on my claim for [describe the loss — for example: storm damage to my roof on (date)]. Your letter dated [date of denial or payment letter] [denied the claim / paid $___]. I disagree for these reasons: [state each reason plainly — for example: the estimate attached shows $___ in repairs; the policy section you cited covers this type of damage].

Please review the claim again and send me a written explanation of your decision, including the exact policy language you rely on. I have attached [photos / contractor estimate / receipts / other evidence]. Please send any further requests for information to me in writing at the address above.

Please respond by [date — from your policy or the insurer’s letter]. If this is not resolved, I will contact [your state]’s insurance department and consider my other options.

Sincerely,
[Your full name]
[Phone number] [Email address]

Not legal advice These are general templates, not legal advice and not a substitute for a lawyer. Laws differ by state and change over time, and a letter that fits one situation can hurt another. If the other side has a lawyer, threatens court, or the amount is large, talk to a licensed attorney in your state or your state’s free legal help before you send anything.
