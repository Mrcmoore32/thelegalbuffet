Lemon law in Florida

Which vehicles qualify

3+ repair attempts for the same defect (with a final repair opportunity after written notice), or 30+ cumulative days out of service, within the 24-month lemon law rights period

Remedy

What you get

full purchase-price refund or replacement vehicle

Filming the police: your rights in Florida

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

All-party consent

State law generally requires everyone recorded to consent — but on-duty officers in public have no reasonable expectation of privacy, so courts have held that recording them is protected. (Fla. Stat. § 934.03)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — in effect

25-foot HALO law (Fla. SB 184, 2024) — in effect since Jan. 1, 2025

Section 934.03 only protects conversations with a real expectation of privacy, which on-duty officers in public do not have — the 11th Circuit (Smith v. City of Cumming, 2000) protects filming them. Under the HALO law, after a verbal warning you must stay 25 feet back from a first responder on duty, but the law itself says peaceful audio/video recording is a legitimate purpose and not harassment by itself.
