Filming the police: your rights in Alaska

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Alaska Stat. § 42.20.310(a))

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Alaska has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

A 2025 governor-backed bill (SB 85) would have switched Alaska to all-party consent, but it stalled in committee and never passed. The 9th Circuit (Fordyce v. City of Seattle) protects filming police doing their jobs in public.

Key facts — Alaska divorce

Residency
No minimum number of days — you must be domiciled in Alaska at filing: physically present with intent to remain (official court form instructions; AS 25.24.090)
Waiting period
30 days for dissolution: the hearing happens at least 30 days after the petition is filed (DR-10 instructions)
Filing fee
$250 for a family law case (Administrative Rule 9)

Sources: AS 25.24.090; Alaska Admin. Rule 9
