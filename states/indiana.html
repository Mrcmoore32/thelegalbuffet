Filming the police: your rights in Indiana

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Ind. Code § 35-33.5-5-5)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — in effect

25-foot law (Ind. Code § 35-44.1-2-14, HEA 1186) — BLOCKED, not in effect

Indiana is one-party consent. Its 2023 25-foot buffer-zone law was preliminarily enjoined in Sept. 2024 and the Seventh Circuit upheld the block in Aug. 2025 (Reporters Comm. v. Rokita), finding it unconstitutionally vague. The 7th Circuit (Alvarez) also protects recording police as First Amendment activity.

Key facts — Indiana divorce

Residency
6 months in Indiana, plus 3 months in the county where you file
Waiting period
60 days after filing before the case can be finalized (per statute IC 31-15-2-9 — confirm with the court)
Filing fee
$157–$177, varies by county ($157 statewide base civil fee; $185 with sheriff service; some counties add a $20 ADR fee)
