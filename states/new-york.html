Filming the police: your rights in New York

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (N.Y. Penal Law §§ 250.00, 250.05)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

New York has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

New York's Right to Record Act (Civil Rights Law § 79-p, 2020) lets people sue if officials interfere with recording police activity. The 2nd Circuit (Massimino v. Benoit, 2026) recognized a clearly established First Amendment right to record police in public going forward. The NY Court of Appeals (Reyes, 2026) held there is no right to film inside police station lobbies, and an NYPD policy banning it stands.
