Gun laws in Massachusetts

Gun laws vary a lot from state to state — what's perfectly legal in one state can be a crime in another. Here's the big picture for this state. Laws change often, so double-check anything before you act.

Concealed carry

Carrying a concealed handgun

Permit required (License to Carry); may-issue with suitability discretion, age 21

Open carry

Carrying openly

Generally allowed with a License to Carry

Buying a gun

Background checks & waiting periods

Private sales: Required for all private transfers (purchaser's license verified via state Gun Transaction Portal) No waiting period.

Extreme risk protection

Yes. Courts can temporarily order firearms removed from someone found to be a danger.

Filming the police: your rights in Massachusetts

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

All-party consent

State law generally requires everyone recorded to consent — but on-duty officers in public have no reasonable expectation of privacy, so courts have held that recording them is protected. (Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 272, § 99)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Massachusetts has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

Secret audio recording of anyone (including police) is a crime, but merely making the recording obvious — e.g., holding a phone up — satisfies the law since only actual knowledge, not formal consent, is required. Glik v. Cunniffe, 655 F.3d 78 (1st Cir. 2011), held openly filming police in public is protected by the First Amendment. Secretly recording officers during a traffic stop was upheld as a crime in Commonwealth v. Hyde, 434 Mass. 594 (2001).
