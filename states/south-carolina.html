Filming the police: your rights in South Carolina

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (S.C. Code Ann. § 17-30-30(C))

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

South Carolina has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

The 4th Circuit (Sharpe v. Winterville Police Department, 2023), which covers South Carolina, held that recording and livestreaming your own traffic stop is protected speech, and the right is now clearly established in the circuit. Interfering with officers (S.C. Code Ann. § 16-9-340) is still punishable — the protection covers recording, not getting in the way.
