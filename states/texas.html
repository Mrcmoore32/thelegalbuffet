Lemon law in Texas

Bought a new car that's constantly in the shop? Texas's lemon law may get you a refund or a replacement.

Coverage

Which vehicles qualify

new and leased vehicles within 2 years/24,000 miles (also used vehicles still under warranty)

Threshold

When it's legally a lemon

4+ failed repairs of the same defect (2 for serious safety hazards) or 30+ days out of service within 24 months/24,000 miles

Remedy

What you get

replacement vehicle, refund, or cash settlement

Filming the police: your rights in Texas

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Tex. Penal Code § 16.02)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — in effect

none in effect (a 2015 proposal, HB 2918, to criminalize filming police within 25 feet was dropped and never enacted)

Turner v. Driver, 848 F.3d 678 (5th Cir. 2017): the First Amendment protects recording police in public, subject to reasonable time, place, and manner limits. Officers can still order bystanders back for safety under the interference-with-public-duties law (Tex. Penal Code § 38.15).
