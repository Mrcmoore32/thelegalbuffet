Filming the police: your rights in Mississippi

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Miss. Code Ann. § 41-29-531)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Mississippi has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

One-party consent state: a person who is a party to a communication (or has one party's consent) may lawfully record it. No Mississippi law restricts filming on-duty police in public; Turner v. Driver, 848 F.3d 678 (5th Cir. 2017), protects recording police as First Amendment activity.
