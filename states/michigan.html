Gun laws in Michigan

Gun laws vary a lot from state to state — what's perfectly legal in one state can be a crime in another. Here's the big picture for this state. Laws change often, so double-check anything before you act.

Concealed carry

Carrying a concealed handgun

Permit required (Concealed Pistol License); shall-issue, age 21

Open carry

Carrying openly

Generally allowed without a permit (18+; heavily restricted in vehicles without a CPL)

Buying a gun

Background checks & waiting periods

Private sales: Universal background checks required (all firearms, since Feb 2024) No waiting period.

Red flag law

Extreme risk protection

Yes. Courts can temporarily order firearms removed from someone found to be a danger.

Out-of-state permits

Reciprocity

Recognizes all valid out-of-state resident permits.

Filming the police: your rights in Michigan

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Mich. Comp. Laws § 750.539c)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Michigan has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

Michigan's eavesdropping statute is written broadly, but courts have long applied a 'participant exception' — if you are a party to the conversation you may record it (Sullivan v. Gray, 117 Mich. App. 476 (1982)). Filming on-duty police in public is treated as First Amendment-protected; no Michigan law specifically restricts recording officers.
