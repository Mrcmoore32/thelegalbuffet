Lemon law in North Carolina

Bought a new car that's constantly in the shop? North Carolina's lemon law may get you a refund or a replacement.

Coverage

Which vehicles qualify

new passenger cars, pick-up trucks, motorcycles, and most vans

Threshold

When it's legally a lemon

4+ repair attempts for the same nonconformity or 20+ business days out of service in any 12-month warranty period; defect within 2 years/24,000 miles

Remedy

What you get

comparable new replacement vehicle or refund, at the consumer's option

Filming the police: your rights in North Carolina

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-287)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

North Carolina has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

The 4th Circuit (Sharpe v. Winterville Police Department, 2023), which covers North Carolina, held that recording and livestreaming your own traffic stop is protected speech. Filming itself is not obstructing an officer (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 14-223), but an officer can still enforce orders aimed at genuine interference.
