Lemon law in Rhode Island

Bought a new car that's constantly in the shop? Rhode Island's lemon law may get you a refund or a replacement.

Coverage

Which vehicles qualify

new and leased vehicles (motor vehicles under 10,000 lbs); used vehicles also covered

Threshold

When it's legally a lemon

4+ repair attempts for same defect or 30+ calendar days out of service within 1 year/15,000 miles

Remedy

What you get

refund or replacement vehicle

Filming the police: your rights in Rhode Island

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-35-21)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Rhode Island has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

The 1st Circuit (Glik v. Cunniffe, 2011), which covers Rhode Island, held there is a clearly established right to openly record on-duty police in public; Gericke v. Begin (1st Cir. 2014) extended it to traffic stops. The right does not protect physically interfering with officers, only recording.
