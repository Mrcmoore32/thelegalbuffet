Gun laws in Rhode Island

Gun laws vary a lot from state to state — what's perfectly legal in one state can be a crime in another. Here's the big picture for this state. Laws change often, so double-check anything before you act.

Concealed carry

Carrying a concealed handgun

Permit required; hybrid shall-issue (towns) / may-issue (Attorney General); minimum age 21

Open carry

Carrying openly

Allowed with restrictions (requires AG-issued license)

Buying a gun

Background checks & waiting periods

Private sales: Universal background checks required Waiting period: 7 days.

Red flag law

Extreme risk protection

Yes. Courts can temporarily order firearms removed from someone found to be a danger.

Out-of-state permits

Reciprocity

Does not honor any out-of-state concealed carry permits.

Filming the police: your rights in Rhode Island

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-35-21)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Rhode Island has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

The 1st Circuit (Glik v. Cunniffe, 2011), which covers Rhode Island, held there is a clearly established right to openly record on-duty police in public; Gericke v. Begin (1st Cir. 2014) extended it to traffic stops. The right does not protect physically interfering with officers, only recording.

Key facts — Rhode Island divorce

One year in Rhode Island (RI Gen. Laws § 15-5-12) — 2025 bills proposed cutting this to 6 months, but enactment is not verified, so check whether this has changed
