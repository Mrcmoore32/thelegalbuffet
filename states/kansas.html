Filming the police: your rights in Kansas

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (K.S.A. § 21-6101)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — law on the books

25-foot 'Halo Act' buffer law (HB 2372) — enacted April 2026 via veto override; enforcement/challenge status unverified

Kansas is one-party consent; the state supreme court confirmed it in State v. Roudybush (686 P.2d 100, 1984). The Tenth Circuit (Irizarry v. Yehia, 2022) held there is a clearly established First Amendment right to record police in public. The 25-foot buffer law for first responders is very new and press groups called it likely unconstitutional; no court ruling on it was found.
