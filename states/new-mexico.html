Car accident injuries in New Mexico

A car crash can turn your life upside down in seconds. Here is the practical rundown for New Mexico: what to do first, how the insurance system works, and your deadline to act.

Right after the crash: call 911, get a police report, exchange information with the other driver, take photos, see a doctor even if you feel okay, don't admit fault, and notify your own insurer promptly.

New Mexico’s system: New Mexico is an at-fault state: the driver who caused the crash — and their insurance — is responsible for your injuries.

Deadline: In New Mexico you generally have 3 years from the crash to file an injury lawsuit. Miss it and you lose the right to sue.

Cost: Most car-accident lawyers work on contingency — free consultation, and they only get paid if you win, out of the settlement.

Filming the police: your rights in New Mexico

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (N.M. Stat. Ann. § 30-12-1)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

New Mexico has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

The 10th Circuit (Irizarry v. Yehia, 2022), which covers New Mexico, held the First Amendment protects filming on-duty police in public and that the right was clearly established by 2019. Simply recording from a safe distance is not obstructing an officer (N.M. Stat. Ann. § 30-22-1), but physically interfering still can be.
