Filming the police: your rights in Louisiana

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (La. Rev. Stat. § 15:1303)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — in effect

25-foot law (La. R.S. § 14:108.1.1) — BLOCKED, not in effect

Louisiana is one-party consent. The 2024 25-foot buffer law took effect Aug. 1, 2024, but a federal judge (M.D. La., Jan. 31, 2025) preliminarily enjoined enforcement as unconstitutionally vague; the state's appeal was pending in the Fifth Circuit as of 2026. The Fifth Circuit held in Turner v. Driver (2017) that a First Amendment right to record police exists.
