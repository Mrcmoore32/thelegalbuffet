Filming the police: your rights in Wisconsin

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Wis. Stat. § 968.31)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Wisconsin has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

ACLU v. Alvarez, 679 F.3d 583 (7th Cir. 2012), held the First Amendment protects openly recording police performing duties in public. In August 2025 the 7th Circuit (which covers Wisconsin) struck down Indiana's 25-foot police buffer law as unconstitutionally vague.
