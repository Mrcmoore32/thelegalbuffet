Lemon law in Arizona

Bought a new car that's constantly in the shop? Arizona's lemon law may get you a refund or a replacement.

Coverage

Which vehicles qualify

new vehicles primarily; limited used-car protection (15 days or 500 miles after purchase)

Threshold

When it's legally a lemon

4+ repair attempts of the same problem or 30+ days out of service within 2 years or 24,000 miles

Remedy

What you get

replacement vehicle or full refund of purchase price

Tenant rights in Arizona

Renting comes with rights most people never hear about until something goes wrong. Here are the key ones in Arizona.

Eviction

Notice before eviction

30 days to end month-to-month; 5 days to pay or quit for nonpayment

Security deposit

Deposit limits & return

Max 1.5 months' rent; return within 14 days (excluding weekends/holidays) with an itemized list

Repairs

When the landlord won't fix things

May repair and deduct after 10 days' written notice for minor defects, capped at $300 or half a month's rent (whichever is greater)

Filming the police: your rights in Arizona

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (A.R.S. § 13-3005)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — in effect

8-foot buffer law (HB 2319) struck down permanently — not in effect

The 2022 law that made it a misdemeanor to film police within 8 feet was permanently blocked by a federal judge in July 2023 as unconstitutional; the state declined to defend it. The 9th Circuit's Fordyce v. City of Seattle also protects filming police in public.
