Filming the police: your rights in Utah

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Utah Code Ann. § 77-23a-4(7)(b))

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Utah has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

Utah law says recording an officer performing official duties in plain view is not, by itself, interference with a peace officer (Utah Code § 76-8-305(4)). Irizarry v. Yehia, 38 F.4th 1282 (10th Cir. 2022), held the First Amendment right to film police in public is clearly established.

Key facts — Utah divorce

Residency
One spouse must be a bona fide resident of the filing county for at least 90 days before filing (Utah Code § 81-4-402(1)(a))
Waiting period
30 days between filing and the judge signing the decree, waivable only for extraordinary circumstances
Filing fee
$350 for "Divorce or Separate Maintenance" (official court fee schedule, fees effective May 6, 2026)

Sources: Utah Code §81-4-402(1)(a); Utah state courts fee schedule
