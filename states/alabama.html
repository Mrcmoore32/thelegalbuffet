Lemon law in Alabama

Bought a new car that's constantly in the shop? Alabama's lemon law may get you a refund or a replacement.

Coverage

Which vehicles qualify

new vehicles only (Ala. Code §8-20A-2)

Threshold

When it's legally a lemon

3+ repair attempts for the same defect plus a final repair attempt by the manufacturer, or 30+ cumulative days out of service, within 24 months/24,000 miles

Remedy

What you get

comparable new replacement vehicle or full refund, at the consumer's option

Filming the police: your rights in Alabama

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Ala. Code § 13A-11-30)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Alabama has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

You can record a conversation you are part of without telling anyone. On-duty police in public have no expectation of privacy, and the 11th Circuit (Smith v. City of Cumming, 2000) protects filming them.
