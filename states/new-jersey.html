Filming the police: your rights in New Jersey

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (N.J.S.A. 2A:156A-4(d))

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

New Jersey has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

One-party consent state: any participant may record a conversation without the others knowing. Fields v. City of Philadelphia, 862 F.3d 353 (3d Cir. 2017), which covers New Jersey, recognizes a First Amendment right to record police performing their duties in public. No buffer-zone law exists.

Civil rights violations in New Jersey

If a police officer, employer, landlord, or other authority treats you worse because of your race, sex, disability, religion, or another protected trait, that’s a civil rights violation — and you can report it. Deadlines are short: many complaints must be filed within 180 days to a year of what happened, so don’t wait. Read the full Constitution here.
