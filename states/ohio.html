Filming the police: your rights in Ohio

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Ohio Rev. Code § 2933.52)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — in effect

15-foot law (H.B. 20, signed July 7, 2026) — in effect since Oct. 6, 2026

People may not approach or remain within 15 feet of a first responder after a verbal warning, but only when they also interfere with the responder's duties or threaten physical harm — simply observing or recording is not prohibited on its own. The 6th Circuit has no binding decision, but federal district courts in Ohio treat the right to openly film police as clearly established (Higginbotham v. City of Cleveland, N.D. Ohio 2015).
