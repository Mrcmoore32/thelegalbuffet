Filming the police: your rights in Wyoming

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 7-3-702)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Wyoming has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

No consent is needed to record conversations in public where there is no reasonable expectation of privacy. Irizarry v. Yehia, 38 F.4th 1282 (10th Cir. 2022), which covers Wyoming, held the First Amendment right to film police in public is clearly established.

Civil rights violations in Wyoming

If a police officer, employer, landlord, or other authority treats you worse because of your race, sex, disability, religion, or another protected trait, that’s a civil rights violation — and you can report it. Deadlines are short: many complaints must be filed within 180 days to a year of what happened, so don’t wait.

Federal report

DOJ Civil Rights Division

Report civil rights violations — including police misconduct — to the U.S. Department of Justice.

Report a civil rights violation →
Job discrimination

EEOC — file a charge

If an employer discriminated against you, file a Charge of Discrimination with the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission.

File a charge of discrimination →
Housing discrimination

HUD — fair housing complaint

Report housing discrimination to the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.

File a housing discrimination complaint →

Wyoming has no state-level civil rights enforcement agency, so the federal options above are the way to file.
