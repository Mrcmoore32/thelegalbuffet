Filming the police: your rights in Pennsylvania

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

All-party consent

State law generally requires everyone recorded to consent — but on-duty officers in public have no reasonable expectation of privacy, so courts have held that recording them is protected. (18 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 5703)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Pennsylvania has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

Courts have narrowed the wiretap law for police recording: officers performing duties in public have no reasonable expectation of privacy, so recording them is not a wiretap violation (Kelly v. Borough of Carlisle, 3d Cir. 2010). The 3rd Circuit (Fields v. City of Philadelphia, 2017) recognized a First Amendment right to record on-duty police in public.

Civil rights violations in Pennsylvania

If a police officer, employer, landlord, or other authority treats you worse because of your race, sex, disability, religion, or another protected trait, that’s a civil rights violation — and you can report it. Deadlines are short: many complaints must be filed within 180 days to a year of what happened, so don’t wait.
