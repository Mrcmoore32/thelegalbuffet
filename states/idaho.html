Lemon law in Idaho

Bought a new car that's constantly in the shop? Idaho's lemon law may get you a refund or a replacement.

Coverage

Which vehicles qualify

new motor vehicles purchased or leased in Idaho; vehicles transferred while the express warranty is in effect

Threshold

When it's legally a lemon

4+ repair attempts of the same defect, 30+ business days out of service, or 1 failed brake/steering repair likely to cause death or serious injury, within 2 years or 24,000 miles

Remedy

What you get

replacement with a comparable new vehicle or full refund (less a reasonable use allowance)

Filming the police: your rights in Idaho

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Idaho Code § 18-6702(2)(d))

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Idaho has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

Idaho is a one-party consent state; a participant may record without telling others, but recording a conversation you are not part of is barred. No Idaho-specific court ruling on recording police was found, and no state buffer-zone law was found. The First Amendment right recognized by federal courts applies.
