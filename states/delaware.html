Filming the police: your rights in Delaware

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

All-party consent

State law generally requires everyone recorded to consent — but on-duty officers in public have no reasonable expectation of privacy, so courts have held that recording them is protected. (11 Del. C. § 1335(a)(4))

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Delaware has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

Delaware has two recording statutes that appear to conflict (§ 2402(c)(4) reads one-party; § 1335(a)(4) requires all-party consent), and no court has resolved it — treat it as all-party to be safe. Either way, the 3rd Circuit (Fields v. City of Philadelphia, 2017) protects filming police in public.

Key facts — Delaware divorce

Residency
At least one party must have continuously resided in (or been domiciled in) Delaware for 6+ months before filing (13 Del. C. § 1504)
Waiting period
"Separation" means living separate and apart for 6+ months before the ruling (§ 1503(8)) — no separate waiting period beyond that
Filing fee
$175 total ($165 civil filing + $10 court security), per the Family Court Schedule of Assessed Costs effective 7/20/26

Sources: 13 Del. C. §1504; §1503(8); Family Court Schedule of Assessed Costs
