Filming the police: your rights in Tennessee

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Tenn. Code Ann. § 39-13-601)

Distance rules

Buffer-zone law — in effect

25-foot 'PEACE Act' (Tenn. Code Ann. § 39-16-611, SB30/HB55) — in effect since July 1, 2025; statute number per ACLU of Tennessee explainer

After an officer orders you to stop or retreat, knowingly approaching or staying within 25 feet while the officer performs official duties is a Class B misdemeanor, with an affirmative defense if the warning was not received or understood. No binding 6th Circuit ruling recognizes a right to record police in public. The 25-foot law is being challenged in two federal lawsuits (a media-coalition suit and an ACLU suit); both were pending as of fall 2026.
