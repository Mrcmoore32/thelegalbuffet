How divorce filing works in Minnesota

In Minnesota, divorce is legally called a “dissolution of marriage.” Here's the process in plain language:

  1. Check residency. At least one spouse must have lived in Minnesota for 180 days before filing.
  2. File in the right county. You file with the district court in the county where either spouse lives.
  3. Use the court's free tools. Minnesota's courts offer Guide & File, a free online interview that builds your divorce forms for you, plus every form as a free download.
  4. Serve your spouse and follow the steps. The court's self-help pages walk through each phase, including what happens if your spouse doesn't respond.

Filming the police: your rights in Minnesota

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Minn. Stat. § 626A.02, subd. 2(d))

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Minnesota has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

One-party consent state: any participant may record a conversation. Minneapolis's January 2025 DOJ consent decree explicitly orders officers not to interfere with civilians photographing or recording them in public. Some federal sources note the First Amendment right to record police is not yet 'clearly established' in the 8th Circuit, which affects civil suits against officers.
