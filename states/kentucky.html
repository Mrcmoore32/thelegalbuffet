Filming the police: your rights in Kentucky

Federal appeals courts across the country have recognized a First Amendment right to photograph and record police officers performing their official duties in public. The U.S. Supreme Court has not ruled on it directly. The right does not protect interfering with officers, trespassing, or breaking other laws while you record.

Audio recording

One-party consent

If you are part of a conversation, you may record it — including with police. (Ky. Rev. Stat. § 526.010)

Distance rules

No buffer-zone law

Kentucky has no law setting a minimum distance for filming police. You still must not interfere with officers doing their jobs.

Kentucky is one-party consent: 'eavesdrop' is defined as recording without the consent of at least one party. No Kentucky-specific court ruling on recording police and no state buffer-zone law were found.
